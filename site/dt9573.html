Setting up a hub behind a router is the same as setting up DC++ behind a router: you need to forward a (single) port to your hub machine. Typically hubs run on :411, so forward 411/tcp.

Post by Xan1977 » 2004-04-16 22:36

Here's another link www.portforward.com

Here's the basic idea. If you're behind a router you are "NAT'd". You are on your own sub-network which exists outside the internet. Therefore, you need to route particular traffic in to the proper computer on your little sub-network. To get your radio station and hub to be visible to the outside world, you need to forward a port through your public IP to your sub-network IP.

BSOD wrote all about this subject as it applies directly to DC++ and active mode. link.

The same concept appplies to running a hub, webserver, shoutcast server or whatever other services you want to run.

step 5:
Name: DC_HUB
Type: TCP
Start Port: 411
Finish Port: 411

step 8:
Service: DC_HUB
Action: Allows Always
Send to LAN Server: 192.168.0.x (put in your local computer IP)
WAN Users: Any
Log: Never
