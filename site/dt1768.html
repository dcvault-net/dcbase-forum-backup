Post by GargoyleMT » 2003-04-01 17:08

Problem 1: DC++ doesn't know the upload/download limits of your connection. Detecting this is not so easy.

Problem 2: Certain ratios might be viewed as quite abusive still: like 1.5mbit/128kbit DSL. Twenty percent of that is 37.5kbyte/s down and 3.2kbyte/s up, which I don't think many people would view as "fair."

I agree that this is a potential solution, and the one that many softwares take, but it's not a particularly good solution.

An aside is that the official NMDC client v1.1 for OS X has upload limiting included, and limits the download speed to 8x the upload speed. That's not a particularly good solution either.

There are upload limited clients out there, I don't see how you can truthfully say "... so at this time it's not possible for me to share"

Post by Wjierd » 2003-04-15 16:09

I have to agree that upload control in some form is a good idea. I think a reasonable solution is trusting people not to lie about their connection and using OPs to punish blatant abusers. I know this isn't a perfect solution, but it would help people whose upload bandwidth is too crammed to allow significant DLs to get much greater useability. Of course, some form of effective automated control would be better, but would be much more difficult to accurately implement in my opinion. I also know a significant portion of users only get a limited amount of bandwidth per month and they end up uploading much more than they download because their ULs kill their DLs. I'm not one of these users, fortunately for me, but it's easy to understand how this issue is also a serious concern for them.
