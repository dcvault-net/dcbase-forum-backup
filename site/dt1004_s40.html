I have no argument with anyone expressing an opinion. I have not only acknowledged Arne's "right" to mess with his own work, but have exprssed my gratitude to Arne for the work he has done, saying he is "an asset."

I simply, and firnly, disagree with the use of a software tool as a medium for political editorial. I feel that it is, in essence, the author (artiste?) ;=]
introducing intentional "bugs" in his/her software under the guise of "fixing" bugs, just to express an opinion. There are more appropriate methods to express one's opinion. This all started with me issuing an open statement to Arne that I disagreed with his choice. It escalated far beyond my intent.

As was mentioned before, you can start a thread, but you can't control it's direction once it's started. Any political commentary I've posted has been in response to others' reactions. I've tried to stay "on message."

IMO, it would have been equally inappropriate had Arne added a clip of the SouthPark "Osama Bin Laden Has Farty Pants" episode. It still woudn't be appropriate use of the medium. I may have laughed, but I still wouldn't have liked that it was done.

Anarchy has to have its limits in order to keep from degenerating into chaos, and those limits need to be based on honor and integrity. Both personal honor/integrity and the integrity and honor one displays to the world in their work, and what they do with it.

If Arne wants to write a goofy-ass program whose only purpose is to, in an "artistically and esthetically pleasing tour-de-force of programming genius," express his political opinions, he has that right.

He has the right to do what he did, as well. My position, the premise for my original post, is that I disagreed with what he did, for what it does to the integrity of his creation of DC++, and the implication of how such application of "programmer's license" could adversely affect the direction of open-source software.

Too many respondents couldn't look past their own political prejudices far renough to recognize that my point was not war in Iraq, or whether or not George W is a cowboy, or who is smarter than whom.

Either they reacted in knee-jerk fashion, blasting Bush and US foreign policy, or they went ga-ga over Arne's "right" as "an artist" to do whatever he wants with his work of art (or the paint on his siding, or the bumper stickers on his car - one of those, anyway) - especially if they agreed with the opinion he chose to express. That's like the asswipe bigots of the 1960's telling the blacks to "go back to Africa" if they don't like their lot in the US... Takes real genius to come up with such a display of logic.

There is a valid difference of opinion, somewhere, under the crap, that goes to the root of the integrity of open-source software as a creation, an intellectual product, an art form; and whether the "personal integrity" of a programmer should trump the integrity of his/her product for the short-term satisfaction of their need to express their political beliefs. I don't think so. Some of you do. Some agree, at least in part, with my argument. Some don't care about that, they just want to wrap themselves in the flag or put daisies in gun barrels, depending on their political ideology, regardless of the original intent of the post.
